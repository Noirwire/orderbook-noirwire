<h1 align="center">orderbook-noirwire</h1>
<p align="center">An order book for spot and perpetual markets with a private book, running inside MagicBlock's Private Ephemeral Rollup, with its TypeScript client.</p>

Orders rest and match inside a private rollup. Other traders and the public cannot read the book, the ledger of seats or another trader's view, except that a trader can find each side's best price inside the crossing band with a few post-only orders ([DESIGN.md](docs/DESIGN.md) section 1, limit 4); the rollup operator can see, order, delay or drop transactions inside the rollup. The tape of fills, the mark prices, the market settings and the lifetime counters are public. Every trading instruction is signed by a one-time key, so an observer who lists signatures cannot link two orders to each other or to a wallet by their signer; fills, timing, deposits and withdrawals can still link activity ([DESIGN.md](docs/DESIGN.md) section 1).

The repository has five parts:

- `docs/` is the contract: [DESIGN.md](docs/DESIGN.md) (accounts, keys, instructions, custody, trust), [RULES.md](docs/RULES.md) (the money rules) and [SPIKE.md](docs/SPIKE.md) (what was measured on the rollup). Change the documents first, then the code.
- `crates/engine` is the matching and risk engine: pure Rust, no I/O, no heap, no clock. It owns every money rule in `RULES.md`.
- `programs/noirwire-orderbook` is the Anchor program: accounts, authority, permissions, custody, the per-trader view, and the calls into the engine. It reimplements no rule.
- `sdk/` is the client, `@noirwire/orderbook`: addresses, instruction builders, account decoders, the one-time order key manager, receipts, sign-in, and a `placeOrder` that confirms from the trader's own view.
- `mcp/` is `@noirwire/orderbook-mcp`, an MCP server that lets an AI agent trade on the private book on the Solana devnet test network, with test money. It uses the client and refuses any network but devnet.

The operator console lives in its own repository, console-noirwire.

`tests/` proves the program through the built client on a local three-part network. `ops/` sets up a deployment and shares its helpers with the tests. `spike/` is the measurement code behind `SPIKE.md`, kept as it ran.

We publish this source so that anyone can read what runs. See [LICENSE](LICENSE) for what you may do with it.

## What it guarantees, and what it does not

Checked by the program and the engine, and proven by the tests on a local network:

- Tokens leave custody only through `withdraw`, signed by the seat's owner, to a token account that owner controls, and only within margin. A deposit credits a seat in the same instruction that moves the tokens. After every step of the suite, custody equals the sum of the seats (RULES 13.1).
- Only one of a view's four order keys can place, cancel, sync, liquidate or move value between the seat's own balances; each key is replaced by the one the instruction names, so a transaction that ran cannot run again while its key stays replaced, and a client that never names a key twice, as this one does, gives no two instructions the same signer. That is about linkability, not about theft: whoever holds a live key names the next one, so a leaked key stays usable until the owner replaces all four (`SECURITY.md`). The owner key cannot trade, and an order key cannot withdraw, close or replace keys.
- Every trading instruction carries an expiry. The program refuses it, before anything else, once the rollup's clock is past it or when it is more than 60 seconds ahead; the key stays live either way. The expiry bounds when an instruction may execute, not when it was signed.
- A refusal that depends on the contents of a book is an outcome in the trader's view, never an error: a post-only order that would match, a full side, a fill the fill check, the position cap or the open interest cap stops, an immediate-or-cancel with nothing to match, a cancel of an order that is gone, and a liquidation that did nothing. Errors say only what the caller could know from public settings and their own seat. A liquidator is not told why there was nothing to liquidate: no such seat, no position, not below maintenance and a price beyond its worst price are one recorded value.
- The collateral token, a registered token's mint and custody account, and a market's tokens never change, so no setting can redirect which custody account a balance is paid from. A market returns to active status only through `resume_market`, and only while no market has a recorded shortfall and its own price source is healthy. A market's first mark, its first `sync_price` from Pyth or, on a test network's unbound market, the admin's reset (never the oracle's publish), leaves it reduce-only, or paused, until resumed.
- A deposit names its beneficiary by owner key and cannot reach the fee seat or the insurance seat; only the admin funds insurance. The exchange opens no more seats in a UTC day than its `max_seats_per_day`.
- The ledger and every book carry a permission with no members; a view carries one whose only member is its owner. A stranger, an anonymous caller and the owner of another seat read nothing from them through the private endpoint, and through it a transaction that touches them shows no data, logs or result, not even to its sender.
- An account created inside the rollup is refused until it has its full size and is finalised. Every load checks owner, derivation, tag, size, readiness and, for a market's accounts, the market id.
- Only the program's upgrade authority can set up the exchange. Only the admin can change settings, register tokens, create and finalise markets, change limits, pause, reset a price, schedule funding, and move the exchange; the admin role moves in two steps and the old admin keeps nothing.

Not this program's to guarantee:

- **Confidentiality of reads** is the rollup's query filter. The program attaches the permissions; the rollup enforces them. The rollup's own port serves everything to anyone who can reach it (the tests read the ledger there to check invariants). On a hosted validator that port is the operator's promise.
- **Custody visibility, and deposits through a private endpoint.** A token is registered with sealed or public custody, an explicit choice that never changes afterwards. Sealed: `register_token` refuses a token whose custody balance lacks a private permission no member can read through. Public: it refuses one whose custody balance has any permission. The local filter (0.1.3) and MagicBlock's hosted devnet endpoint (measured 2026-10-09, the table in `spike/devnet/README.md`) both refuse every transaction of a program other than the token programs that names a private token balance. With sealed custody, `register_token`, deposits and withdrawals are therefore sent to the rollup's own port, which only the local network has; that address is `depositUrl` in the deployment description (`DEPOSIT_URL` in the Makefile). Devnet has only the hosted endpoint, so devnet uses public custody (`DEVNET_CUSTODY` in the Makefile; `custodyVisibility` per token in the description), and a depositor's or withdrawer's own token balance must be public too.
- **What public custody shows.** Anyone reads each token's total in custody, and so the size and time of every deposit and withdrawal as that total changes, and the depositor's or withdrawer's own token balance when that account is public. It does not show resting orders, book depth, seat balances or positions, and, since the endpoint serves no transaction contents, not which seat was credited or debited. `SECURITY.md` has the detail and one open risk.
- **The upgrade authority** can replace the program and is part of the custody trust model. It is not the exchange's admin, and changing one does not change the other.
- **Ordering and timeliness.** The rollup operator can see, order, delay or drop transactions inside the rollup, and under load the rollup executes accepted transactions late (`SPIKE.md`, follow-up on question 6). The expiry bounds how late an order can fill.
- **The mark price** is Pyth's, read from the one Pyth account each market is bound to by a permissionless `sync_price`, within a move limit; a confirmed jump beyond it is settled at the source (`docs/RULES.md` section 9). The program trusts that the rollup clones that account faithfully and that someone syncs it within `max_age`. On the test networks, whose program is built with the `test-oracle` feature, one oracle key writes the mark of a market bound to no source; the default build mainnet deploys has no such instruction.
- **Withdrawal to Solana** is not part of this program. Tokens live inside the rollup as Ephemeral SPL Token balances.

This program has not had a third-party audit.

### Accounts

All sizes are the program's. An account inside the rollup costs `(size + 60) * 32` lamports of rent, paid by the exchange and returned when it is closed. The figures below follow that formula; the test suite asserts the view's and the permission's, and a local set-up was measured charging the exchange exactly the sum for one ledger, stats and three markets.

"Readable by" is what the rollup's private endpoint serves; "the program only" is a private permission with no members. The rollup's own port serves every account to whoever can reach it, as "Not this program's to guarantee" says.

| Account      | Address                                             | Readable by                                        | Size              | Rent, lamports | Holds                                                                                                                        |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- | -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Exchange`   | `["exchange"]`, on Solana, delegated                | everyone                                           | 596 (Anchor)      | Solana rent    | admin, pending admin, gate, oracle, pause, max steps, collateral token, tokens, the daily seat cap, custody caps, USDC guard |
| `Ledger`     | `["ledger"]`                                        | the program only                                   | 917,528           | 29,362,816     | 2,048 seats (engine state); the fee seat is 0 and the insurance seat is 1                                                    |
| `Stats`      | `["stats"]`                                         | everyone                                           | 160               | 7,040          | orders, fills, volume and open interest per market                                                                           |
| `Market`     | `["market", id]`                                    | everyone                                           | 160               | 7,040          | engine `MarketParams`, symbols, book capacity per side                                                                       |
| `Book`       | `["book", id]`                                      | the program only                                   | 131,160           | 4,199,040      | 1,024 resting orders per side, sequences, funding index, what traded since the last funding update                           |
| `Tape`       | `["tape", id]`                                      | everyone                                           | 28,712            | 920,704        | ring of the last 512 fills, last price                                                                                       |
| `PriceFeed`  | `["price", id]`                                     | everyone                                           | 176               | 7,552          | the mark and its two times, the bound Pyth account and scaling, the last observation and incident                            |
| `TraderView` | `["view", owner]`                                   | that owner only                                    | 3,736             | 121,472        | owner, four order keys, seat index, ring of 16 results, seat copy with open orders on one market                             |
| permission   | permission program, per account                     |                                                    | 68 or 101         | 4,096 or 5,152 | the member list: none for a sealed account, the owner for a view                                                             |
| custody      | an associated token account of `["custody", nonce]` | the program only when sealed, everyone when public | SPL token account | paid on Solana | every token the seats hold; sealed by a private permission, or public, as registration records                               |

Every rollup account starts with a 16-byte header: an 8-byte tag, a ready flag, the market id, the bump, and padding. The body is the engine's `#[repr(C)]` struct, mapped zero-copy. An account above 10,240 bytes is created at that size and grown by `grow_account` in 10,240-byte steps, up to 30 per transaction; it is refused until `finalize_ledger` or `finalize_market` has checked its size and set the flag. A step here is one `grow_account` after the account is created: the ledger needs 89, in 3 transactions after the one that creates it; a book 12; a tape 2.

Every order-key instruction takes `expires_at` (unix seconds), the replacement key, a client order id the result is filed under, and the market id, and names the same accounts: the view, the exchange, the ledger, that market's market, book, price feed and tape, and the stats, with the other perp markets as remaining accounts, three per market (market, book, price feed). So the public accounts' signature lists do not tell one order-key instruction from another (`docs/DESIGN.md` section 1). `place_order`, `liquidate`, `withdraw` and `transfer_between_balances` check each of those markets like the market being traded, so a cross-margin account is valued over every market it holds a position on; `transfer_between_balances` changes nothing on the market it names. `reconcile_shortfall`, `resume_market` and `collect_fees` take the market account of every perp market, by rising id, and refuse a partial list; `set_seat_access` takes the book of every perp market the same way, whatever seat it names.

Errors are one enum with stable codes: 6001 to 6099 are the engine's own, one to one; this program's begin at 6100. The interface file is `target/idl/noirwire_orderbook.json` after a build.

### Outcomes

Through the private endpoint, a transaction that touches a sealed account shows nothing, not even to its sender, so every order-key instruction writes an `OrderResult` into the view's ring of 16 (newest first in the decoder). For a placed order the status is the engine's `PlaceStatus` code: filled (1), rested (2), remainder cancelled (3), step limit (4), book full (5), post-only would match (6), fill check (7); `order_seq` is the resting remainder's sequence number, to cancel by, and means nothing when `rested` is zero. For a liquidation it is liquidated (1), stale price (5), liquidator margin insufficient (7) or nothing to liquidate (8). The last stands for the engine's target seat not open, no position, not liquidatable and worst price exceeded, written with every number zero, so an attempt does not tell a liquidator whether a seat exists or holds a position; nothing about the target ever becomes an error. A liquidation adds nothing to the public volume or fill counters; it moves public open interest when the liquidator's own position offsets what it takes over. A transfer between balances reports done (0) with the amount in `filled`. A cancel or sync reports done (0); a cancel of a gone order reports refused (100) with the engine code in `code`. Fills are appended to the public tape with the two receipts of RULES 10, and the client recognises its own by recomputing them from its order secrets.

## The client

`sdk/` builds to `sdk/dist` as ES modules and packs to `sdk/noirwire-orderbook-<version>.tgz` (`make sdk`, which keeps only the current version's file), the file an app's `package.json` points at by URL. The client runs in Node 24 and in a browser; it imports nothing from Node. Dependencies, pinned exactly: `@solana/web3.js` 1.99.0, `@solana/spl-token` 0.4.15, `@magicblock-labs/ephemeral-rollups-sdk` 0.17.3, `@noble/hashes` 1.8.0. Neither the client nor the MCP server is published to npm.

- `Addresses` derives every address. `Instructions` builds every instruction with the accounts in the program's order; the suite checks each one against the interface file.
- `decodeMarket`, `decodeTape`, `decodePriceFeed`, `decodeStats`, `decodeView`, `decodeLedger`, `decodeExchange` read account data with integers as `bigint`.
- `OrderKeyManager` derives order key `i` from a 32-byte seed as `Keypair.fromSeed(sha256("noirwire-orderbook/order-key/v1" || seed || i as u64 LE))`, keeps the four live keys, lends one out with its replacement, and recovers its position from a view: `restore` from a `checkpoint` the app saved (a few derivations), or `fromView`, which searches from index 0 and is for a device with no checkpoint. A slot stays lent to its call until that call settles it against the key the view shows, so concurrent calls never share a key.
- `randomSecret`, `receipt` and `ownFills` are RULES 10.
- `privateConnection(url, owner, signMessage)` signs in to the private endpoint and returns a connection that reads as the owner.
- `TraderClient.placeOrder` signs with a one-time key and an expiry (5 seconds by default, at most 60), sends, and confirms from the view's result ring, resending the same signed bytes every second until the expiry. The device's clock only decides when to stop waiting: with no result by then the outcome is `unknown`, because the order may still run under the rollup's clock, and its order key and client order id stay out of use. `settled` on that outcome resolves to `placed` or, once the rollup's own clock is two seconds past the expiry and the view shows no result, to `expired`; only then is the key lent again. A result counts only if it was written after the call began, with the call's client order id and kind. The id is always the client's own 64 random bits, held by no other call in flight and by no result in the ring, and is returned as `clientOrderId`. An order off the tick or below the market's minimums is refused before signing (`OrderInvalid`). Execution is never read from `getSignatureStatuses`; it is asked once, after 400 ms without a result, only to learn that the transaction failed, which is thrown as `TransactionFailed` instead of being waited out. Results carry `sentAt` and `resultAt` in monotonic milliseconds. Everything is sent with preflight skipped. `cancelOrder`, `cancelAll`, `syncView`, `liquidate` and `transferBetweenBalances` work the same way, except that they throw `OutcomeUnknown`, which carries the same `settled` promise, where `placeOrder` returns `unknown`. A call whose reads fail after it was sent throws `OutcomeUnknown` too, with the failure as its `cause`.
- A call costs one request, its send. From its first call (or from `ready()`, which waits for all three) the client keeps in the background: a blockhash, fetched every 15 seconds and never for a call; the rollup's clock as an offset from the device's, measured every five minutes and whenever the clock is read anyway, which the expiry is counted from unless `now` is given, at most 58 seconds ahead; and the trader's own view, pushed over a websocket account subscription to the reader's endpoint with the reader's sign-in token. The result is taken from that subscription, also when it arrives before the send has answered. The view is read instead while the subscription is not established or is down, and once it has delivered nothing for `pushWaitMs` (500 ms). A dropped socket is opened again after a wait that doubles from 250 ms to five seconds, subscribed again and the view read once more, so no write is missed; `renewReader(connection)` does the same with a newly signed-in reader. A send refused with "blockhash not found" is signed and sent once more with a new blockhash, and an instruction refused as `Expired` or `ExpiryTooFar` under the estimated clock is tried once more after reading the clock. `new TraderClient(..., { push: false })` subscribes to nothing and reads the view for every result. A client that has made a call holds a websocket and a timer: `close()` ends both, and a Node process that never calls it does not end by itself.
- `MarketReader` reads and subscribes to the tape, a price feed and the stats; `TraderClient.subscribeView` to the own view.
- `setupLedger` and `setupMarket` run the multi-transaction creation and are safe to repeat.

## Build and test

You need Rust (the version in `rust-toolchain.toml` is picked up by itself), the Solana CLI 2.3.11, Anchor 1.2.1 and Node 24 or later. The Anchor CLI and the `anchor-lang` crate are both 1.2.1. With `avm`, `make` uses `anchor-1.2.1` directly whatever version is active, and refuses to build with any other version: run `avm install 1.2.1`. Anchor 1.2.1 builds for SBPF v3 by default, which the local validators do not load, so `make build` asks for `--arch v0`.

```sh
make install        # npm ci, for the tests and the client
make build          # the test networks' program and its interface file
make build-mainnet  # the program mainnet deploys and its interface file, under target/mainnet/<id>; DECLARED_ID=<id> for another program id
make check-mainnet  # build-mainnet, then prove its binary and interface file have no instruction that writes a price and declare that id
make test           # build, build the client, run make unit, start a fresh local network, run the suite, stop it
make unit           # the instruction builders, the client against a fake connection, and what the ops scripts decide; no network, after make build
make check          # cargo fmt, clippy with warnings as errors in both builds, prettier, tsc for the client, the tests and ops
make format         # cargo fmt and prettier, writing
make audit          # cargo audit over Cargo.lock
make sdk            # build the client and pack the release .tgz, removing older ones
make mcp            # build the client, then the MCP server to mcp/dist
make up / down      # the local network in the background, for the local-* targets
make local-setup    # exchange, ledger, three markets, two mints, custody, faucet; prints JSON; safe to repeat
make local-status   # what is deployed there
make local-smoke    # two kept traders deposit, fill and cancel there; counts and times orders
make clean          # remove .localnet with its throwaway keys, the build leftovers and the packed client
```

`make local-access`, `make rehearsal-access` and `make mainnet-access` set the access lists as the profile's admin, chosen by `ACCESS`: `show` prints each perp market's maker and taker list switches; `ACCESS=market MARKET=<id> MAKERS=on|off TAKERS=on|off` sets them and reads the market back.
`ACCESS=seat SEAT_OWNER=<key> MAKER_MARKETS=<ids|none> TAKER_MARKETS=<ids|none> BLOCKED=yes|no` sends `set_seat_access` with every perp book and a 1,400,000 compute budget on every call, and prints the signature; a seat's lists are sealed, so they are not read back. It simulates the call first and sends nothing when the simulation fails, such as for an owner with no open seat, since a failed call lands in the exchange's public signature list with its error.
`make mainnet-access` refuses without `MAINNET_PROGRAM_ID`, like the other mainnet targets, and every command that sends checks the admin key is the exchange's admin first.

`make local-fund` and `make rehearsal-fund` (`OWNER=<key> AMOUNT=<whole quote tokens>`, `ops/fund.ts`) fund a trader's seat where there is no faucet: the admin mints the test quote token and deposits it as collateral to `OWNER`'s open seat, and prints the signature (the new collateral too, where this machine holds the owner's key).
It stops if the seat is not open, if the deposit would pass the custody cap (raise it with `CUSTODY_CAP` on the setup target; this never does), or on a mainnet genesis, for which there is no target.

`make local-faucet` and `make rehearsal-faucet` (`AMOUNT=<whole quote tokens>`, `ops/faucet.ts`) give a network with no faucet one: the key `<network>-faucet.json` (made if missing, never printed) is topped up to `AMOUNT` of the test quote token, minted by the admin, and recorded as `faucet` in the description, with every other field unchanged.
It prints the custody cap the grants need (they count against it; this never raises it) and the key file the test service reads, refuses a quote token the admin cannot mint, and refuses a mainnet genesis, for which there is no target.

`make devnet-setup`, `make devnet-status` and `make devnet-smoke` are the same three against devnet (see "Deploying").

`make test` starts a Solana validator, a private rollup and its query filter on ports 8899, 7799 and 6699 (websockets one port up), runs every test against them and stops them. Nothing reaches a public network. The program is loaded at its declared address with a throwaway key under `.localnet` as its upgrade authority. `make test-upgrade UPGRADE_FROM=<file>` starts the network with an earlier build's program instead, runs the story on it, deploys this build over it at the same address with that key, and then runs the access lists' audit on the state the earlier build left, so it proves an in-place upgrade reads that state. The earlier build is `target/deploy/noirwire_orderbook.so` after `make build` at the commit to upgrade from.

### The local deployment

`make local-setup` against a running `make up` network sets up what the other services expect and prints a description they read, also written to `.localnet/deployment.json`: the program id, the exchange, ledger and stats addresses, the gate, oracle and faucet public keys, the two test mints (nUSD, 6 decimals, and nSOL, 9 decimals) with their decimals, custody nonces and custody accounts, the address deposits and withdrawals are sent to (`depositUrl`), and three markets with their four addresses, base and quote decimals, lot size and tick each. Each market's first mark is the admin's reset, sent with `resume_market` in one transaction, since the oracle cannot set a first price. The faucet key under `.localnet/localnet-faucet.json` holds 10,000,000 nUSD and 100,000 nSOL inside the rollup, so test money comes from an ordinary deposit by that key, sent to `depositUrl`. Each token's custody nonce is drawn at random and kept in `.localnet/localnet-custody-nonce-<symbol>.json` until the token is registered; setup stops with a message naming that file when someone else set up that custody first. The exchange is created with the client's default of 100 new seats a day; the admin raises it with `update_exchange`.

| Market       | Kind | Lot        | Price unit         | Initial mark       | Max leverage | Maintenance | Penalty | Taker fee | Crossing band | Max move | Funding                          |
| ------------ | ---- | ---------- | ------------------ | ------------------ | ------------ | ----------- | ------- | --------- | ------------- | -------- | -------------------------------- |
| `NSOL-PERP`  | perp | 0.001 SOL  | nUSD atoms per lot | 150,000 ($150/SOL) | 10x          | 5%          | 2%      | 5 bps     | 4%            | 2.5%     | every 60 s, at most 0.5% per 8 h |
| `NNVDA-PERP` | perp | 0.01 share | nUSD atoms per lot | 1,800,000 ($180)   | 10x          | 5%          | 2%      | 5 bps     | 4%            | 2.5%     | every 60 s, at most 0.5% per 8 h |
| `NSOL-NUSD`  | spot | 0.001 SOL  | nUSD atoms per lot | 150,000            |              |             |         | 5 bps     | 4%            | 2.5%     |                                  |

These satisfy RULES 12 (penalty plus fee below maintenance, band and funding cap within the margin gap, and one price update plus one interval of funding at the cap leaving a seat that was at maintenance more than the penalty, long or short). Half of each penalty and half of each perp taker fee go to the insurance seat; spot fees go wholly to the fee seat; the open interest cap is 10,000,000 lots and the position cap a fifth of it, 2,000,000 lots per seat. Each token's custody cap is the faucet's whole supply, so on a test network it refuses nothing the faucet handed out; `SECURITY.md`, "Running a small mainnet market", has the settings a real market would start with. Funding on the perps is scheduled with the rollup's built-in scheduler, which calls `update_funding` every 60 seconds signed by the validator at no cost. The oracle must publish at least every 10 seconds (`max_price_age`; liquidations tolerate 60), at most once a second, or orders that increase exposure and withdrawals by position holders are refused as stale.

## Deploying

[docs/LAUNCH.md](docs/LAUNCH.md) is the mainnet launch and its day-to-day, step by step, and says how to run the crank and the keeper from the container image (`make image-amd64`, `make image-arm64`). Nothing here deploys by itself, and nothing in this repository has run on mainnet; the `mainnet-*` targets refuse to unless `MAINNET_PROGRAM_ID` names the program they send to. The `devnet-setup`, `devnet-status` and `devnet-smoke` targets run the same scripts against devnet with keys under `.keys` (git-ignored; `devnet-admin.json`, the upgrade authority, is put there by hand). On devnet the exchange is delegated, the ledger is sealed, the two test tokens are registered with public custody and the three markets are ready; `make devnet-smoke` deposits, fills, cancels and then counts the requests and times thirty resting and thirty crossing orders there, with the result pushed and with it polled for, and `make local-smoke` does the same against a local deployment with sealed custody. A stale price makes `update_funding` do nothing and succeed rather than fail, so the hosted rollup's scheduler, which stops a task after one failed run, keeps advancing funding on its own. The exchange records each token's custody nonce, which changed its layout from 415 to 451 bytes, the USDC guard, which took it to 564, and each token's custody cap, which took it to 596; the market account grew with the Pyth price source and the position cap, and the price feed with the Pyth price source. There is no migration: neither this program nor the client reads an exchange created by an earlier build, such as the one on devnet, so do not upgrade the program over it. Devnet has to be set up again from scratch, under a new program id since the exchange's address is fixed by it, with fresh `.keys/devnet-deployment.json` and token registrations; the test money in the old custody stays there. Before real money: the upgrade authority on a multisig, the price-source soak, keepers and insurance of `SECURITY.md` ("Running a small mainnet market"), and a settled answer to withdrawal back to Solana (`DESIGN.md` section 6 and 7).

**Rehearsing the mainnet launch.** The mainnet profile (`PROFILE=mainnet`; the steps in `ops/network.ts`, the settings in `ops/deployment.ts`) sets up the market of `SECURITY.md`, "Running a small mainnet market", on the program `make build-mainnet` builds. `make rehearsal-setup` runs it on devnet at the program id of `.keys/rehearsal-program.json`, with `.keys/rehearsal-admin.json`, its upgrade authority, as admin; it writes `.keys/rehearsal-deployment.json` and leaves the devnet deployment alone. The program deployed there must be, byte for byte, the build `make check-mainnet DECLARED_ID=<that id>` puts under `target/mainnet/<id>`, which declares that id from a copy of the sources; every rehearsal and mainnet target checks this before it sends anything. Every target names the genesis it expects (`GENESIS`, `local` for the local validator), only `NETWORK=mainnet` runs on mainnet's, and the mainnet profile runs on mainnet and the rehearsal only. Each step is skipped once done: the exchange at 5 new seats a day (`SEATS_PER_DAY` on the rehearsal); the quote token (`QUOTE_MINT`, or a test mint of USDC's decimals made under `.keys`) with public custody and a custody cap of 0; the ledger; `SOL-PERP` bound to Pyth's SOL/USD, or, when it exists already, checked to have exactly the launch's settings and source; the USDC guard bound to Pyth's USDC/USD and synced; the first `sync_price`; funding; 3,000 USDC of insurance from the admin; the custody cap set to `CUSTODY_CAP` whole tokens, which must exceed insurance plus every deposit, since the cap counts the whole custody account; the keeper's seat, opened and funded with `KEEPER_DEPOSIT`, which is required; and, last, the mark printed beside the reference price, the median of the SOL/USD last-trade prices of Coinbase, Kraken and Bitstamp (each source and its price are printed), and what insurance covers at it, and `resume_market` only on a run with `RESUME_MARKET=yes`, the two within 1% and the open interest cap covered at that mark (on mainnet, also only once the admin is no longer the upgrade authority, and only once the keeper's seat is funded and the keeper is seen running: the run watches the keeper's view for a write, up to `KEEPER_EVERY` plus a minute of backoff plus 30 seconds, so the first run funds the seat, the keeper is started, and a second run with `RESUME_MARKET=yes` resumes; a resume that is refused for the keeper fails the run); when it is not covered, the refusal names the cap to lower `open_interest_cap` to with `update_market`, and setup takes caps at most the launch's. Once the market has opened, a run leaves the daily seat cap, the USDC guard and the market's status as it finds them and says which differ from the launch, since the admin may have changed them in an incident; `RESTORE=seatsPerDay`, `quoteSource` or `resume` names the one a run writes back, and `RESUME_MARKET=yes` no longer does anything. Funding, insurance, the opening of the keeper's seat and the keeper's deposit are recorded in `.keys/<network>-sent-<program id>.json` under an id before they are sent; one an earlier run left pending stops setup until `FUNDING_SENT`, `INSURANCE_SENT`, `KEEPER_SEAT_SENT` or `KEEPER_DEPOSIT_SENT` says `landed:<id>` or `lost:<id>` for that record, and setup never signs with the keeper's order keys, so it cannot take them from a running keeper, and with public custody insurance is not sent while custody already holds it. One setup run at a time holds a lock file with the keys, and a run whose gate key is not the exchange's stops first. `make rehearsal-crank` reads every bound Pyth account every `CRANK_EVERY` seconds, 1 by default (10 with `CRANK_PUSH`), sends `sync_price` and `sync_quote` for each newer observation, logs the rollup clock minus its publish time, and counts a market it cannot read as a failure. Devnet's own Pyth updates come every one to five minutes, too slowly for the 90 and 180 second ages, so `CRANK_PUSH=1` first posts Hermes's updates through Pyth's push oracle at mainnet's cadence, paid by `.keys/rehearsal-crank.json`. Hermes answers only a caller with a Pyth Core API key, given as `HERMES_TOKEN`; without one the push stops with a 401. The mark check does not use Hermes and needs no key: it asks Coinbase, Kraken and Bitstamp's public tickers at once (fixed addresses, 10 seconds each), skips a source that fails and says why, needs at least two answers, and takes their median; with two answers the reference is their mean, and the check stops if they are more than 1% apart. A Pyth Pro token, a different key from `HERMES_TOKEN`, is optional: given as `PYTH_PRO_TOKEN` (the rehearsal and mainnet targets read it from `.keys/pyth-token` when that file exists), it adds Pyth Pro (`https://pyth.dourolabs.app`, the feed named by its Pro id in `ops/deployment.ts`) as one more source in the same median, and a token Pyth Pro refuses only skips that source. `PYTH_PRO_URL` and `HERMES_URL` name other servers on test networks only. The rehearsal's Solana RPC is `REHEARSAL_SOLANA_URL`, devnet's public one by default, which rate-limits a launch. The crank also logs each market's status and price health and the guard's state, and counts a round that leaves an incident open as a failure. `make rehearsal-keeper KEEPER_SEATS=<n>` sweeps liquidations blind from the keeper's seat over every seat below `n`, the highest seat index the gate has opened plus one with the two reserved seats counted, plus one day's openings, and grows that bound with every opening the exchange counts, kept across restarts in `.keys/<network>-keeper-seats.json`; it stops after `KEEPER_GIVE_UP` failed sweeps, or once one market has read stale for `KEEPER_STALE_GIVE_UP` seconds (1,800). `make rehearsal-smoke` deposits, fills, cancels and checks privacy at Pyth's mark, with nothing writing a price, and however it ends cancels both traders' orders and closes their positions. The `mainnet-*` targets run them against mainnet with real USDC, and refuse without `MAINNET_PROGRAM_ID=<the program id>`, `MAINNET_ROLLUP_URL` and `MAINNET_VALIDATOR`, and unless that rollup serves mainnet's USDC mint; there the quote mint must be USDC's, `PYTH_PRO_URL`, `HERMES_URL` and `CRANK_PUSH` are refused, and the smoke asks for its own `MAINNET_SMOKE=yes`, only tops each trader's seat up to its deposit, and never raises a custody cap.

**Drilling price incidents.** `make rehearsal-drills` (`ops/drills.ts`) makes the rehearsal's market meet a price incident on purpose, proves the program does what `RULES.md` says, and undoes it, so the market is left active with the USDC guard and the price source bound as launched; each check prints an ok, FAIL or SKIP line, and the run fails on any FAIL and also on a SKIP that leaves a drill's claim unproven, which it names on a `NOT PROVEN` line. What a drill changed is put back even when the drill stops; a restore that fails says how the market is left and stops the run there. `guard-trip` binds the guard with `depeg_bps` 1 and proves that while it fails closed, unread and then depegged, an order that grows a position is refused with `QuoteGuardTripped` and a reduce-only order is taken, then binds the launch's settings again and proves the guard passes (section 9a); a guard synced by someone else around a check makes that check a skip. `stale-price` sends no `sync_price` until the mark is older than the market's max age, proves an increasing order and a withdrawal by a seat with a position are refused with `StalePrice`, then syncs and proves orders are taken again (section 9, Freshness); a mark that gets newer meanwhile means a crank is running, and makes it a skip. Stop the crank before `guard-trip` and `stale-price`: its `sync_quote` and `sync_price` would undo what they set up. `rebind` closes the smoke traders' positions against each other, pauses the market, calls `set_price_source` with the launch's feed, proves the mark, source record and incident are cleared and, where the endpoint serves the book, the funding totals too (section 7), syncs a first mark, checks it as setup does before `resume_market`, and resumes. They trade as the smoke's traders, so `make rehearsal-smoke` runs first; `DRILLS=guard-trip,rebind` picks drills by name; a drill that needs a Pyth observation waits up to six minutes for one; and they refuse a mainnet genesis in code as well as having no `mainnet-*` target.

## Security

Every instruction checks who signed, which program owns each account, that each address is the one its seeds derive, and that each writable account was passed writable. Arithmetic is checked and the release build keeps overflow checks on. The contact for reports is embedded in the program with `solana-security-txt`. What a leaked order key can and cannot do, and the rest of the threat model, is in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).
